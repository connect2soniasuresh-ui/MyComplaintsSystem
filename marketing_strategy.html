1. Executive Summary and the Prototype

1.1 Executive summary

Grievance Desk is a white-label complaints website and staff console for banks. A customer lodges a complaint about any banking service, gets a reference number straight away, and tracks it. The bank's complaints officers work one shared queue in which every case shows a 30-day clock, matching the RBI rule that a bank must give its final reply within 30 days before the customer can go to the RBI Ombudsman. Our prototype runs as “Sangam Bank”, a fictional demo bank. Each customer bank gets its own branded instance.

What sets it apart is security and trust by design. The form refuses OTPs, CVVs, PINs, full card numbers, Aadhaar and PAN, checking once in the browser and again in the database. Fraud complaints are flagged urgent automatically and the customer is told to block their card. Staff see contact details masked, and every reveal is logged. Data is stored in India. On top of this sit two AI features. The AI Complaint Assistant lets a customer simply describe the problem in a chat and turns that into a complete complaint. AI routing and escalation sends each complaint to the right team and escalates it automatically before Day 30.

The beachhead is India's urban cooperative banks (UCBs), small finance banks (SFBs) and regional rural banks (RRBs). They face the same RBI grievance expectations as large banks, but have small IT teams, no enterprise CRM, and often run complaints on paper registers, email and Excel.

Strategy in one line: Give a smaller bank a complaints desk it can trust and launch in under two weeks. It lets customers complain just by chatting, uses AI to route every case to the right team and escalate it before Day 30, and keeps customers' secrets out of the system.

GTM motion: account-based, founder-led sales through cooperative-bank federations, core-banking vendors and compliance consultants, backed by a 60-day pilot. Business model: annual SaaS subscription by bank size, plus a one-time setup fee. Launch: week of 18 January 2027, with 3 pilot banks live and 2 paying banks by 31 March 2027.

1.3 Where AI fits

Built with AI: the prototype was designed and coded with an AI coding assistant on Supabase (hosted Postgres) and GitHub Pages. That's why a small team could build a production-grade, security-hardened app in weeks, and that speed is part of our cost advantage. AI in the product comes from two features, both built into the prototype. They run in a server-side function that calls Claude (Anthropic), so the API key never reaches the browser. 1. AI Complaint Assistant (chatbot)		The customer types the problem in their own words, for example “I paid ₹2,500 by UPI on 2 Oct, money left my account but the shop didn't get it”. The assistant identifies the service and issue (UPI, debit-but-not-credited), pulls out the date, amount and UTR, asks only for what's missing, and shows a summary. Built
2. AI routing and auto-escalation		Routing: AI reads each complaint and sends it to one of nine teams (Cards & ATMs, Payments & UPI, Digital banking, Loans & recovery, Accounts & deposits, Branch operations, Insurance & investments, KYC & records, Fraud cell), using the service, the issue, the amount and the wording, and rates its risk as low, medium or high. It also catches likely fraud the customer filed under the wrong category. Escalation: a rule-based matrix, informed by the AI risk rating, escalates cases automatically. A fraud case, or one the AI rates high risk, goes to the team lead straight away, and fraud of ₹1 lakh or more goes to the Principal Nodal Officer. A case with no action for 7 days goes to the team lead. A case at Day 20 without a resolution goes to the nodal officer. Day 25 goes to the Internal Ombudsman or GM. A customer's third complaint in 90 days goes to the Principal Nodal Officer. Levels never go down. Every routing and escalation is logged in the case history with the reason.Complaints reach the right officer on day 1, not day 5. Breaches of the 30-day limit are prevented before they happen, and the escalation chain RBI expects is followed automatically and recorded.

Safeguards (as built): the chatbot never sees the customer's name, mobile or email, because the customer types those into the form; messages containing an OTP, PIN, CVV, card, Aadhaar or PAN number are blocked before they reach the model; the AI routes each complaint only once and staff can re-route; rule-based routing and fraud flags apply even if the AI is unavailable; each user is rate-limited; every AI decision is recorded in the audit trail. Later roadmap: a weekly AI insight brief for the board and AI-drafted replies for officers.

2. Market Context and Problem

2.1 Why now

  • Complaints keep rising. RBI's Ombudsman annual reports show complaints to RBI Ombudsman offices rising every year, to about 9 lakh+ in FY2023-24. Digital payments, loans, cards and mobile banking are the biggest categories, and those are exactly the 12 services our form covers.
  • The 30-day rule is now visible to customers. Under the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (2021), customers can escalate if the bank doesn't reply within 30 days. Every complaint that reaches the Ombudsman is a visible failure, can lead to a compensation award, and goes into the bank's supervisory record.
  • RBI is watching smaller banks more closely. Cooperative banks have come under tighter supervision. Supervisors expect a documented grievance process, a nodal officer and complaint disclosures.
  • Fraud and phishing. Customers often put OTPs and card numbers into complaint emails, and fraudsters pose as bank staff. A complaints channel that refuses secrets protects the customer and the bank.
  • DPDP Act, 2023. Data minimisation, purpose-limited consent and access logging are becoming legal requirements, and our prototype already has them.

Source: RBI, Annual Report of the Ombudsman Schemes 2023-24 (figures rounded).

5.4 Communication approach

  • Tone: calm, plain-language and trustworthy, like a senior compliance officer, not a tech startup. The product itself is written in simple English (“Tell us what went wrong”), and our marketing should sound the same.
  • Show it, don't describe it: the strongest sales moment is typing an OTP into the form and watching it refuse. Build every demo, video and webinar around that moment and the 30-day meter.
  • Trust through peers: cooperative bankers buy what other cooperative bankers use. Federation endorsement and pilot-bank testimonials are the most important assets.
  • Responsible AI: “AI assists, a human decides.” The chatbot runs through the same confidential-data guard and never asks for an OTP or PIN. Officers can override any AI routing decision. Every routing and escalation is logged with its reason, and customers' contact details never go to the AI.
  • Vernacular: customer-facing collateral (posters, SMS) in Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati, Tamil and other languages for each pilot bank's region.

6. Pricing and Business Model

6.1 Business model

B2B white-label SaaS, annual subscription paid by the bank, priced by bank size (number of branches) with a generous complaint allowance, plus a one-time setup fee. End customers never pay.

Why price by branches: cooperative-bank boards understand and budget by branch count. It is stable and predictable, which avoids the bank worrying that “more complaints means a bigger bill”. It also tracks value, since more branches mean more complaints and more regulatory exposure. Per-user pricing would discourage banks from giving every officer and branch manager access.

6.2 Pricing rationale

  • Value created: one complaints clerk costs a UCB about ₹4–5 lakh a year. Grievance Desk removes manual register and Excel work, cuts “where is my complaint” calls, and helps avoid Ombudsman escalations, compensation awards and adverse inspection findings. We estimate ₹6–10 lakh a year of value for a 25-branch bank.
  • What customers pay elsewhere: generic helpdesks cost ₹1–3 lakh a year but need configuration and lack banking controls. Core-banking change requests and enterprise suites cost ₹10 lakh+.
  • Cost to serve: static hosting and hosted Postgres cost roughly ₹2–5k a month per bank, plus AI API costs of under ₹1 per complaint. That allows about 80% gross margin at prices cooperative banks can approve.
  • Price point: capture about 30–40% of the value, for an ROI of 3x or more and a price below the cost of one clerk.

9.3 Key risks and mitigation

RiskImpactMitigation
Cooperative banks are conservative and slow, and need board approvalHighFederation endorsement; free pilot; board-note template; peer references
Vendor-risk and IT outsourcing reviewsHighSecurity pack prepared in advance; India hosting; row-level security; data processing agreement; plan for a third-party security audit
Customers keep using branches and phoneMediumCustomer launch kit with QR codes; branch staff lodge complaints for walk-in customers on the same form, so every complaint is recorded
AI routes a case wrongly or the chatbot misunderstands a customerMediumOfficers can re-route with one click (corrections improve the model); rule-based fraud flags and the 30-day escalation rules still apply even if the AI fails; the chatbot shows a summary for the customer to confirm, and the classic form is always available
Core-banking vendors build their own versionMediumPartner with them instead (revenue share); keep a lead on privacy controls and the customer-facing experience
DPDP and RBI rules changeLow–mediumCompliance lead monitors circulars; rules live in the database layer so changes can be made quickly

9.4 Beyond launch

  • Product: AI-drafted replies (passed through the confidential-data guard), a weekly AI insight brief for the board, regional-language forms, WhatsApp intake, predictive scoring of cases likely to breach the 30-day deadline, RBI-format disclosure reports.
  • Markets: NBFCs and digital lenders (Year 2), insurance (IRDAI's Bima Bharosa flow) and brokers (SEBI SCORES), then other markets with regulator-led grievance rules.
