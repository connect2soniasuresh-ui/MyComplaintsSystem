## Part 1. The product

### What it is

**Grievance Desk**: a white-label complaints website and staff console for India's smaller banks (urban cooperative banks, small finance banks, regional rural banks). The demo bank is the fictional **Sangam Bank**. It is the Group 5 capstone for *Product Management with AI* (IIM Calcutta).

Core flows:

1. **Lodge.** A customer picks one of 12 services and about 50 issues, or describes the problem to the AI complaint assistant, which fills in the form. They get a reference number like `GD-261017-K7QZ8M2P`, the 24-hour resolution target and the Day 30 final-reply date.
2. **Track.** Reference number plus registered mobile shows status and history, and lets the customer reply when the bank asks for information.
3. **Staff queue.** Officers sign in (invitation only) and see tiles, filters, a clock per case against its own 24 or 48-hour target (then Day X of 30), routing team, allocated officer and escalation level. They open a case, reveal contact details (logged), re-route it and send updates. Team leads and above can reallocate a case and change its target.
4. **Monthly report.** The staff console builds a monthly MIS summary (received, resolved, % within 24 hours and 30 days, average time, by service and team). It can be downloaded as CSV or printed to PDF for the board and RBI returns.

### Product rules built into the code

- **Never weaken the confidential-data guard.** `GUARDS` in `app.js`, `private.sensitive_kinds` in `schema.sql` and the check in the Edge Function must stay in step. Any new free-text field gets `data-scan` in the HTML and an `assert_clean` call in SQL.
- **No direct table access.** Add new data access as a checked `security definer` function in `schema.sql`, never as an RLS policy that exposes rows.
- **Contact details stay masked** for staff unless revealed through `reveal_contact` (which logs the view). Exports and reports use the masked values.
- **The AI never sees contact details** and never decides alone. Rules route first. AI may re-route once, and every AI decision is logged with its reason and can be overridden by staff.
- **Two clocks.** Each complaint's `targetHours` (24 or 48) is the bank's own resolution target and drives the case clock, the meter and the time-based escalations, which scale with it: no action for T/6 → team lead, open T/2 → Principal Nodal Officer, open 5T/6 → Internal Ombudsman / GM (4/12/20h for 24 hours, 8/24/40h for 48). At T it is recorded as a breach (`breached_at`) and the customer is told once. `SLA` (30 days) is RBI's legal deadline and drives the reply-by date and the Ombudsman route. Don't merge them.
- **Targets: rules set, AI tightens, people extend.** `private.rule_target` / `ruleTarget()` give 48 hours only to the `COMPLEX_ISSUES` list; fraud and a customer's third complaint in 90 days are always 24. The receipt shows that target. AI may tighten 48 → 24 on its own but can only *suggest* 24 → 48; only a team lead or above can extend (the customer is told). Officers can never change their own target.
- **Allocation is by rules.** `private.pick_officer` gives a complaint to the on-duty `officer` in its team with the fewest open complaints (under `max_open`), ties to whoever was given one least recently. Re-routing reallocates. Unallocated complaints are retried by `run_escalations`. Officers can only take unallocated complaints; leads and above can reallocate. Every allocation is logged.
- **Escalation levels only go up.** The rules in `private.escalation_target` / `private.check_escalation` and `escalate()` in `DemoAPI` must match, as must `rule_target` / `ruleTarget()` and `pick_officer` / `pick()`.
- **Escape all customer text** with `esc()` before inserting HTML.
- **Keep the CSP** in `index.html` tight. Scripts come only from this site and the pinned, integrity-checked Supabase library.
- **Cache-busting:** bump the `?v=` on `styles.css`, `config.js` or `app.js` in `index.html` when you change them.
- **Plain language** in all customer-facing text. The audience includes rural and less tech-savvy customers.

### How personal and confidential data is protected

- **No direct table access.** Row-level security is on and there are no policies, so neither visitors nor signed-in users can read or write the tables. Everything goes through the checked functions in `schema.sql`.
- **Customers prove ownership** with reference number + registered mobile. After 5 wrong mobile numbers, tracking for that reference locks for an hour. Reference numbers have 8 random characters, so they can't be guessed.
- **Confidential details are rejected twice**, in the browser and again in the database: OTP, CVV, PIN, passwords, full card numbers (Luhn-checked), Aadhaar, PAN and full account numbers. Staff messages that ask for these are blocked too.
- **Least exposure for staff.** Mobile and email are masked. Revealing them is a deliberate click, logged in `contact_access_log` with who and when.
- **Short staff sessions.** Sessions are kept in `sessionStorage` (gone when the tab closes), and staff are signed out after 15 minutes without activity.
- **Browser hardening.** A Content Security Policy allows scripts only from this site and the pinned, integrity-checked Supabase library. All customer text is escaped before display.
- **Data minimisation.** Only the last 4 digits of an account or card are accepted, email is optional, there are no uploads, and only reference numbers (never mobile numbers) are remembered on the customer's device.

### The AI features and how to turn them on

Two features run in the `ai` Edge Function (`supabase/functions/ai/index.ts`), which calls Claude. The Claude API key lives only in Supabase, never in the website.

- **AI complaint assistant.** On the Lodge tab, "Describe it in a chat" lets a customer explain the problem in their own words. The assistant asks follow-up questions and fills in the form. The customer still adds their own name and mobile, ticks consent and submits, so contact details never go to the AI. Messages with an OTP, PIN, CVV, card, Aadhaar or PAN number are blocked before they reach the model.
- **AI routing and auto-escalation.** Each complaint is routed to a team by rules the moment it's lodged. The AI then reads it once and may re-route it (for example a "login problem" that is really a scam call goes to the Fraud cell), mark it as possible fraud and rate its risk. The escalation matrix in the database raises cases automatically:

  | Rule | Escalated to |
  |---|---|
  | Fraud complaint, or AI rates it high risk, or no action for 4 hours | Team lead |
  | Open 12 hours without a final reply, fraud of ₹1 lakh or more, or third complaint from the same customer in 90 days | Principal Nodal Officer |
  | Open 20 hours without a final reply | Internal Ombudsman / GM |

  The time rules follow the 24-hour target, so a case reaches the GM before the target is missed. Staff see each case's clock (“5h of 24h”, then “Past 24h · Day 3 of 30”) and a **Past 24-hour target** tile next to **Past 30 days**.

  Every routing decision and escalation is logged in the case history with the reason. Staff can re-route a case from its detail view. Customers don't see these internal entries.

Setup:

1. Get an API key at console.anthropic.com.
2. In Supabase open **Edge Functions → Secrets** and add `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` with your key. Optionally add `ALLOWED_ORIGIN` set to your site's address (for example `https://youraccount.github.io`), so only your site can call the function.
3. Open **Edge Functions → Deploy a new function → Via editor**. Name it `ai`, delete the sample code in the editor, paste all of `supabase/functions/ai/index.ts`, and click **Deploy**. If you use a different name, put it in `aiFunction` in `config.js`. (With the Supabase CLI: `supabase functions deploy ai`.)
4. Escalations run every 10 minutes through `pg_cron`, which `schema.sql` enables. If your project doesn't allow it, escalations still run whenever staff open the queue.

If the function isn't deployed, the site still works: the chat option tells customers to use the form, and complaints stay on rule-based routing.

Each caller is limited to 40 chat messages and 30 routing requests an hour.

### Walkthrough to check the product

There is no automated test suite. Before committing, walk through these in `?demo` mode:

1. Lodge a UPI complaint and get a reference number. Type `OTP 123456` in the description: it must be flagged and block submission.
2. Track it with the reference and mobile.
3. Staff tab (demo signs you in as a team lead): tiles add up, every case shows its officer, the 2-hour KYC case shows “1h of 24h” with no escalation, the 6-hour UPI case is with the team lead (no action for 4 hours), the 30-hour NOC case shows “30h of 48h” with the Principal Nodal Officer, and 24-hour cases older than 20 hours are with the Internal Ombudsman / GM. Open the UPI case, allocate it to another officer and extend it to 48 hours: the customer gets a “little more time” message. Fraud cases can't be set to 48.
4. Open a case, reveal contacts, re-route, send an update. A staff message asking for an OTP must be blocked.
5. Monthly report: totals match the queue, the locker case counts as resolved within target, and there is a By officer table. CSV downloads and opens in Excel. Print preview shows only the report.

### Before using it for real customers

- **Legal review.** Have the privacy notice and data handling reviewed against the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and RBI guidelines. Decide and publish a retention period, and schedule deletion of closed complaints after it.
- **Spam protection.** Add a CAPTCHA (Supabase supports Cloudflare Turnstile and hCaptcha) to stop automated submissions.
- **Staff accounts.** Turn on multi-factor authentication for staff in Supabase.
- **Custom domain.** Use the bank's own domain (GitHub Pages → Custom domain, with HTTPS enforced) so customers can trust the address.
- **Bank name.** The site is branded Sangam Bank; search index.html and app.js for "Sangam Bank" to change it.

#### 1. Summary

Grievance Desk has **two customers**. The **buyer** is a bank, and the person who feels the pain most is its **Principal Nodal Officer**, the officer RBI holds responsible for complaints. The **end user** is the bank's own customer, who needs to complain safely and know what is happening. The product only works if it wins both.

**Our choices, in one paragraph.** We target **Tier 2 and Tier 3 urban cooperative banks (UCBs) with 5 to 60 branches** first, roughly 600 banks, because they face rising RBI scrutiny and the 30-day complaint rule with small IT teams and paper or Excel processes. **Small finance banks and the 28 newly merged regional rural banks** are the second wave. Our primary persona is **Rajesh Kulkarni**, a 46-year-old Principal Nodal Officer at a 28-branch Pune UCB. He is accountable when a complaint goes past Day 30, but he has no live view of his cases (our product puts every case on a 24-hour clock) and spends a late night every month building the board report by hand. On the customer side we design for the **least confident user**: a semi-urban or rural customer, often a fraud victim, who may write in Hindi or Marathi and may be tempted to share an OTP.

**Evidence:** RBI's Ombudsman received **13.34 lakh complaints in FY2024-25, up 13.6%** in a year, and loans, cards, mobile banking and deposits make up about 80% of them. Card and internet fraud is **56% of all bank fraud cases by count**. Only **37.8%** of Indians aged 15+ can do online banking. Each of these findings maps to a feature we built (Section 10).

**Honest status of our research.** This analysis is built on published RBI and government data, on the regulations that shape the buyer's job, and on our product walkthroughs. Our 15 to 20 interviews with nodal officers and CEOs are scheduled in the execution plan (W2 to W4). Every claim below is tagged so readers can see what is proven and what we still need to validate: D published data, R regulation or rule, H team hypothesis to test in interviews.

**Beachhead definition.** RBI-regulated urban cooperative banks in Tier 2 or 3, with 5 to 60 branches, starting in **Maharashtra and Gujarat**, where most UCBs are. Typical profile H: 20 to 150 complaints a week, 1 to 10 staff handling them part-time, a core-banking system from an outside vendor, no CRM, and complaints tracked in branch registers, a shared mailbox and an Excel sheet.

##### 4.1 Why this beachhead wins

- **Highest pain for the size of the team.** The same 30-day rule and inspection expectations as a big bank, but no CRM and a small IT budget R.

- **Best product fit.** Lockers, cheques, deposits and branch service, the services cooperative banks are known for, are all in our form. Large-bank CRMs are overkill.

- **Reachable as a group.** State UCB federations and the national federation (NAFCUB) give us one door to hundreds of banks. Nodal officers' names are public on bank websites.

- **References compound.** Cooperative bankers trust what other cooperative bankers use H, so three pilot banks in one state can unlock the rest.

#### 5. Segmenting the end users: which customers

The bank buys, but its customers decide whether the desk is used. We segmented them by **digital confidence** and **urgency**, the two things that most change what they need from a complaint channel.

| Customer segment | Who they are | What they need | What we built for them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fraud victims** (urgent) | Someone who just lost money to a card, UPI or phishing scam. Anxious and racing the 3-day zero-liability window. | Be told what to do first; be taken seriously at once; not be phished again | Fraud issues auto-flagged urgent and routed to the Fraud cell; “block first, report in 3 days” guidance; AI catches fraud filed under the wrong category |
| **Less confident, vernacular users** | Semi-urban and rural customers, small traders, many older users. Use WhatsApp, not forms. 62% of adults can't bank online D. | Explain the problem in their own words and language; not have to pick from 50 issue types | AI Complaint Assistant replies in the customer's language and fills in the form; plain-English copy; branch staff can lodge for walk-ins |
| **Digital-first payment users** | Urban, UPI-heavy, often younger. Complain about failed or wrong transfers. | Speed, a reference number, status without calling | 2-minute form with UTR field; instant reference; self-service tracking |

**Design rule:** design for the least confident customer and the most anxious one. If a rural fraud victim can lodge safely in two minutes, every other customer can too.

#### 1. Executive Summary

**Grievance Desk** is a white-label complaints website and staff console for India's smaller banks. A customer lodges a complaint about any banking service, by form or by chatting with an AI assistant, gets a reference number straight away and tracks it. AI routes each case to the right team, and an escalation matrix raises it automatically within hours, long before Day 30. A monthly report gives the nodal officer the numbers the board and RBI ask for.

**13.34 lakh**complaints to the RBI Ombudsman in FY2024-25, up 13.6% in a year

**~600**Tier 2 and 3 urban cooperative banks in our beachhead

**56%**of bank fraud cases are card and internet fraud

**₹3.6 lakh/yr**target price for a 25-branch bank, below one clerk's cost

**Why we win:** it is built around RBI's grievance rules, not adapted from a generic helpdesk. #### 2. Strategy and Opportunity

##### 2.1 The problem

India's smaller banks face the same complaint rules as the largest banks: a named nodal officer, a final reply within 30 days, escalation to the RBI Ombudsman, and growing data-protection duties. But they run complaints on **branch registers, a shared mailbox and an Excel sheet**. Nobody has a live view of which cases are close to Day 30. Fraud complaints wait in the same pile as cheque-book requests. Customers email OTPs and Aadhaar copies. At inspection time, staff rebuild the history by hand. The nodal officer, who is personally accountable, finds out a case is late only when it has already escalated.

| Pain today | Consequence | How Grievance Desk fixes it |
|---|---|---|
| Complaints arrive on paper registers at branches, a shared mailbox, phone calls and walk-ins | No single list. Cases get lost and nobody knows how many are open. | One online form, used by customers and by branch staff for walk-ins, and one shared queue |
| No running clock | Breaches are discovered only after the customer escalates | A 24-hour clock on every case (then Day X of 30); auto-escalation at 4, 12 and 20 hours |
| Customers email OTPs, card numbers and Aadhaar copies | Data-protection risk and a target for fraudsters | Confidential-data guard in the browser and the database; no uploads |
| Fraud complaints wait in the general pile | Customers miss the 3-day zero-liability window; losses and anger grow | Automatic urgent flag, Fraud cell routing, block-first guidance |
| “Where is my complaint?” calls | Branch and call-centre time wasted | Self-service tracking with reference number and mobile |
| Board and RBI reports built by hand | An evening a month in Excel; numbers hard to verify | Monthly report with CSV and PDF export |
| No audit trail at inspection time | Days spent rebuilding history from emails and registers | Timestamped history and logged contact reveals |

##### 2.2 Why now

- **Complaints are rising fast.** The RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme received **13,34,244 complaints in FY2024-25, up 13.55%** from 11,75,075, and the disposal rate slipped from 95.1% to 93.1%. Loans (29.3%), credit cards (17.2%), mobile and electronic banking (16.9%) and deposit accounts (16.8%) lead. All four are in our service list.

- **Consolidation and tighter supervision.** The number of UCBs fell to **1,457** by March 2025, with 7 mergers and 8 licence cancellations in the year, under a four-tier regulatory framework. RRBs were merged from 43 into **28** on 1 May 2025. Survivors must show they are well run.

- **Small UCB customers have no RBI fallback.** The Ombudsman scheme covers UCBs only above ₹50 crore in deposits, so for many cooperative-bank customers, the bank's own desk is the only formal route.

- **Digital fraud.** Card and internet fraud made up **13,516 of 23,953** bank fraud cases in FY2024-25. Victims must report within 3 working days for zero liability, so fraud complaints must be found and acted on the same day.

- **DPDP Act, 2023.** Data minimisation, purpose-limited consent and access logging are becoming legal duties. Our product already does all three.

- **AI makes it affordable.** An AI assistant can turn a customer's own words, in their own language, into a complete complaint, and AI-assisted development let a small team build a security-hardened product in weeks. That is what makes a price smaller banks can approve possible.

Aiming at 24 hours means a case reaches Day 30 only if several people have ignored it.

##### 4.3 Where AI fits

**Built with AI:** the product was designed and coded with Claude Code on Supabase (hosted Postgres) and GitHub Pages. That's why a small team could build a production-grade, security-hardened app in weeks, and that speed is part of our cost advantage. **AI in the product** comes from two features, both built into the prototype. They run in a server-side function that calls Claude (Anthropic), so the API key never reaches the browser. Live demo: https://connect2soniasuresh-ui.github.io/MyComplaintsSystem/

| AI feature | What it does | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Built **1. AI Complaint Assistant (chatbot)** | The customer types the problem in their own words, for example “I paid ₹2,500 by UPI on 2 Oct, money left my account but the shop didn't get it”. The assistant identifies the service and issue (UPI, debit-but-not-credited), pulls out the date, amount and UTR, asks only for what's missing, and shows a summary. One click fills in the complaint form; the customer checks it, adds their name and mobile, and submits it to get a reference number. It replies in the customer's language (English, Hindi or another Indian language) and fills in the form in English. Every message goes through the existing confidential-data guard, so if the customer types an OTP or PIN the assistant refuses it and explains why. It will never ask for one. | Removes the hardest part of complaining (choosing among 12 services and about 50 issues), so customers drop off less and complaints are complete. This matters for rural and less tech-savvy customers. |
| Built **2. AI routing and auto-escalation** | **Routing:** AI reads each complaint and sends it to one of nine teams (Cards & ATMs, Payments & UPI, Digital banking, Loans & recovery, Accounts & deposits, Branch operations, Insurance & investments, KYC & records, Fraud cell), using the service, the issue, the amount and the wording, and rates its risk as low, medium or high. It also catches likely fraud the customer filed under the wrong category. **Escalation:** a rule-based matrix, informed by the AI risk rating, escalates cases automatically. A fraud case, or one the AI rates high risk, goes to the team lead straight away, and fraud of ₹1 lakh or more goes to the Principal Nodal Officer. Time rules follow each complaint's own target T of 24 or 48 hours: no action for T/6 goes to the team lead, open T/2 to the Principal Nodal Officer, and open 5T/6 to the Internal Ombudsman or GM (4, 12 and 20 hours on a 24-hour target; 8, 24 and 40 on 48). At T the case is recorded as a breach and the customer is told. **Targets:** the AI also proposes a target. It may tighten a 48-hour target to 24 on its own, but can only suggest extending 24 to 48; a team lead decides, so a promise made to a customer is never stretched by AI. A customer's third complaint in 90 days goes to the Principal Nodal Officer. Levels never go down. Every routing and escalation is logged in the case history with the reason. | Complaints reach the right officer in minutes, not days. Cases are escalated within hours, so breaches of the 30-day limit are prevented long before they happen, and the escalation chain RBI expects is followed automatically and recorded. |

Safeguards (as built): the chatbot never sees the customer's name, mobile or email, because the customer types those into the form; messages containing an OTP, PIN, CVV, card, Aadhaar or PAN number are blocked before they reach the model; the AI routes each complaint only once and staff can re-route; rule-based routing and fraud flags apply even if the AI is unavailable; each user is rate-limited; every AI decision is recorded in the audit trail. Later roadmap: a weekly AI insight brief for the board and AI-drafted replies for officers.

##### 4.4 How it is built

| Layer | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Front end | Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript on GitHub Pages; mobile-first; light and dark themes; strict Content Security Policy | Nothing to install; fast on low-end phones; cheap to host per bank |
| Database and access | Supabase Postgres in Mumbai; row-level security on with no policies; all access through checked security definer functions | Data stays in India; the browser can never read tables directly |
| AI | Supabase Edge Function calling Claude for the chat assistant and routing; API key held server-side; rate-limited | Customers' contact details never reach the AI; rules still work if AI is down |
| Escalations | Rule matrix in the database, run every 10 minutes by pg_cron, which also retries allocation for complaints waiting for a free officer | Escalations, breaches and allocation happen even if nobody opens the queue |
| Development | Built with Claude Code (and Cursor); the project's conventions and safety rules are recorded in CLAUDE.md | A small team shipped a security-hardened product in weeks |

##### 4.5 Scope: what we built, and what we deliberately left out

**In scope and working:** the four flows above, both AI features, the confidential-data guard, routing and escalation, automatic officer allocation, 24 or 48-hour targets with breach tracking, audit logging, the monthly report and the reviewer demo mode. **Deliberately out of scope for now:** SMS and email notifications, regional-language forms (the chat already answers in the customer's language), CAPTCHA and staff MFA (both available in Supabase, to switch on per bank), and core-banking integration. #### 7. Pricing and Business Model

##### 7.1 Business model

**B2B white-label SaaS, annual subscription** paid by the bank, priced by **bank size (number of branches)** with a generous complaint allowance, plus a one-time setup fee. End customers never pay.

**Why price by branches:** cooperative-bank boards understand and budget by branch count. It is stable and predictable, which avoids the bank worrying that “more complaints means a bigger bill”. It also tracks value, since more branches mean more complaints and more regulatory exposure. Per-user pricing would discourage banks from giving every officer and branch manager access.

##### 7.2 Pricing rationale

- **Value created:** one complaints clerk costs a UCB about ₹4–5 lakh a year. Grievance Desk removes manual register and Excel work, cuts “where is my complaint” calls, and helps avoid Ombudsman escalations, compensation awards and adverse inspection findings. We estimate ₹6–10 lakh a year of value for a 25-branch bank.

- **What customers pay elsewhere:** generic helpdesks cost ₹1–3 lakh a year but need configuration and lack banking controls. Core-banking change requests and enterprise suites cost ₹10 lakh+.

- **Cost to serve:** static hosting and hosted Postgres cost roughly ₹2–5k a month per bank, plus AI API costs of under ₹1 per complaint. That allows **about 80% gross margin** at prices cooperative banks can approve.

- **Price point:** capture about 30–40% of the value, for an ROI of 3x or more and a price below the cost of one clerk.

#### Slide 10: Every complaint has an owner and a deadline

**New after review feedback**

##### Automatic allocation

- **Fewest open cases first:** the on-duty officer in the team, within their limit

- **Re-routed?** It is reallocated in the new team

- **Leads reallocate;** officers take unallocated cases

- **By officer** table in the monthly report

##### 24 or 48-hour target

- **Rules set it:** 48 hours when other teams or institutions are needed; fraud always 24

- **AI only suggests** extending; a team lead decides, and the customer is told

- **Escalation scales:** 8, 24 and 40 hours on a 48-hour target

- **Missed target** = breach, with one apology to the customer

Plus “Something else (not listed)” under every service, so customers never pick the wrong issue

> **Speaker notes:** After review feedback we added two things a nodal officer asked for in practice: an owner and an honest deadline for every complaint. Each new complaint is allocated automatically to the on-duty officer in its team with the fewest open complaints. If it is re-routed, it is reallocated. Team leads can move cases; officers can pick up unallocated ones. Each complaint also gets its own target. Most stay at 24 hours, but issues that need another bank, an insurer or a registrar get 48. Fraud is always 24 hours. The AI can tighten a target but can only suggest extending one, so a promise made to a customer is never stretched by AI. Escalations scale with the target, and a missed target is recorded as a breach and the customer is told once. We also added a "Something else" option so customers never have to pick the wrong issue.

#### Scenarios and action plans

**Best case.** *When:* At least 70% of metrics in their best band, none in the worst, no guardrail breached. *What it looks like:* ARR of ₹12 lakh or more, 75% or more of complaints resolved within 24 hours, half of pilots converting, and officers using the console every week.

1. Move new banks to the full Professional tier price and close the founding-bank offer.
2. Open a second state federation and start a regional rural bank pilot.
3. Hire a second customer success person before the number of banks outgrows one.
4. Build voice lodging in regional languages for customers who don't type.
5. Keep the guardrails: the monthly confidential-data audit and reveal-log review continue.

*Owner and review:* PM with the GTM lead; monthly.

**Average case.** *When:* Neither the best nor the worst rule applies. *What it looks like:* Growing, but some metrics stall: 60–75% within 24 hours, payments slow to arrive, and one or two control metrics drifting the wrong way.

1. Take the two weakest metrics and run their fix from the metric definitions for 30 days.
2. Fortnightly review with each bank's Principal Nodal Officer, using the bank table.
3. Push online lodging: QR posters in branches and tracking links in SMS.
4. Move new contracts to annual billing in advance to bring collection time down.
5. Hold new pilots until pilot-to-paid conversion is back at 50%.

*Owner and review:* PM with the CX lead; fortnightly.

**Worst case.** *When:* A guardrail is breached (confidential details stored, or a complaint open past RBI's 30 days), or 4 or more metrics are in the worst band. *What it looks like:* A bank is exposed to RBI or losing trust in the product, revenue stalls and the first cancellations appear.

1. Incident response within 24 hours for any guardrail breach: fix it, tell the bank in writing, write a root-cause note.
2. Freeze new sales and onboarding until the breach is closed.
3. Founder call to every bank this week, then a weekly written update until back to Average.
4. Switch AI routing to rules only if AI errors or overrides are part of the cause.
5. Cut events and ads to protect cash, and re-check the target segment.

*Owner and review:* PM and TECH lead with the whole team; daily until out.

#### 1. Method

| Step | What we did |
|---|---|
| Respondent briefs | For each respondent we wrote a short brief: role, bank type and size, region, tenure, and attitude to technology. Briefs were drawn from the segments and personas in our Consumer and Segment Analysis. At least two respondents were briefed to be sceptical. |
| Interview | A team member asked the questions from our 11-question guide (section 11.2 of the analysis), with follow-ups. The AI answered in character, and was told to push back where a real officer plausibly would and not to agree just to please us. |
| Demo | For the demo questions, the respondent was shown the screens we built: the OTP refusal, the 24-hour clock, escalation and the monthly report. |
| Synthesis | Answers were coded against our seven hypotheses. Every finding is graded “signal to test”, never “confirmed”. |
| Limits | A model can only echo patterns from public material and from our own framing, so it is biased towards what we already believe. Real interviews may contradict all of this, and that is the point of running them. |

#### 2. S1 · Principal Nodal Officer, Tier 3 UCB (simulated)

**Brief:** 51, Principal Nodal Officer and Head of Operations at a 34-branch urban cooperative bank in western Maharashtra. 26 years at the bank. Uses Excel daily. Mildly sceptical of start-ups.

Walk me through the last complaint you handled.

“A customer’s ATM withdrawal failed at another bank’s machine, but the money was debited. He went to the branch, the branch wrote it in the register, and someone emailed my team three days later. We raised it with the switch and it reversed on its own on day 6. But the customer had called the branch four times by then.”

How do you know which complaints are close to 30 days?

“Our Excel sheet has a date column. Every Friday my assistant sorts it. If a branch never sent the complaint to us, it isn’t in the sheet at all. That’s the real problem: the ones I don’t know about.”

Has anyone ever sent you an OTP, card number or Aadhaar copy?

“Every week. People attach a photo of their card, front and back. We delete it, but it sits in the mailbox and in backups.”

How long does your board report take?

“The quarterly one, about a full day, because I check branch numbers against the sheet. Monthly I send a short summary to the CEO, maybe two hours.”

(After the demo) What stands out? What worries you?

“The OTP block, I like a lot. The 24-hour target worries me. A cheque return or a loan NOC can’t be closed in 24 hours. If the screen shows red for half my cases, my staff will stop looking at it. And will RBI see this as our official register? Our auditors will ask.”

Rank the decision drivers.

“Regulatory safety first, then ease for branch staff, then data in India, price, peer recommendation. AI features last. Honestly, AI makes the board nervous.”

#### 3. S2 · CEO, Tier 2 UCB (simulated)

**Brief:** 58, CEO of an 18-branch urban cooperative bank in Gujarat. Former nationalised-bank officer. Price-conscious; the board approves anything above a set limit.

What do you worry about most in grievance handling?

“Inspection findings. Last inspection they wrote that our grievance process was ‘not adequately documented’. It’s a small line, but the board asked me about it for two meetings.”

At what monthly price would this be too cheap to trust, a bargain, getting expensive, too expensive?

“Below ₹5,000 a month, I’d wonder who maintains it. ₹10–15,000 is reasonable for my size. At ₹30,000 I must take it to the board, and they will ask why not the core-banking vendor. Above ₹40,000, no.”

Who else would need to see this?

“The IT committee, the auditor, and our core-banking vendor. If the vendor says it’s a security risk, it’s finished.”

What would make you say yes quickly?

“Another cooperative bank using it. Our federation recommending it. A free trial with no data leaving India.”

#### 4. S3 · Head of Customer Service, small finance bank (simulated)

**Brief:** 42, Head of Customer Service at a small finance bank with several hundred branches. Has a CRM, but the complaint module is old. Digitally confident.

How do complaints reach you today?

“Call centre, branch, email, the app, social media and RBI’s CMS. Our CRM logs most of them, but the branch ones are still patchy. Volume isn’t the problem. Routing is: a microfinance complaint goes to the wrong desk and loses two days.”

(After the demo) Would this replace your CRM?

“No. But the routing logic and the fraud flag are better than ours. If it could sit on top of our CRM and push cases into it, I’d listen. A separate system for staff to check, no.”

The 24-hour target?

“We already have internal turnaround times by category: 24 hours for UPI, 7 days for loans. One number for everything won’t fly with my team.”

#### 5. S4 · General Manager, regional rural bank (simulated)

**Brief:** 55, General Manager (Customer Service) at a regional rural bank formed in the 2025 merger. Many branches, largely rural customers, sponsor bank influence on IT.

What changed after the merger?

“Three banks, three ways of handling complaints. Now RBI and our sponsor bank want one process. It’s a good time to sell us something, but the sponsor bank’s IT team will decide.”

How do your customers complain?

“Mostly in person, in the local language. Many can’t type. A web form alone won’t work. A branch staff member has to be able to lodge it for them, or there must be voice or WhatsApp.”

(After the demo) What stands out?

“The chat in the local language is interesting if it really works in Odia or Bhojpuri, not just Hindi. The monthly report by branch would be more useful to me than by service.”

#### 6. S5 · Complaints officer, Tier 2 UCB (simulated)

**Brief:** 29, one of three people handling complaints at an urban cooperative bank. Also does other back-office work. Daily user of whatever system is chosen.

What takes most of your time?

“Chasing branches. ‘Did you call the customer? What did they say?’ And customers calling me for status. Writing the replies is the easy part.”

(After the demo) Would you use this?

“The queue sorted by urgent and oldest, yes. The escalation to my manager after 4 hours without action, that’s scary. If I’m on leave, does it escalate my cases? Who covers?”

What would make it fail?

“If branches still write in the register and don’t enter it here. Then I have two places to check, and it’s worse than today.”

#### 7. S6 · Customer, fraud victim (simulated)

**Brief:** 47, runs a small shop in a district town. Lost ₹12,000 after sharing a code with a caller claiming to be from the bank. Uses WhatsApp and UPI in Marathi.

What did you do first?

“I went to the branch the next morning. They told me to call a number. That took a day. Nobody told me to block my card immediately.”

(After seeing the form refuse an OTP) How does that make you feel?

“Good. If the bank’s own website stops me, I believe them when they say they’ll never ask. But I wouldn’t have found this website. I would go to the branch or call.”

#### 8. S7 · Customer, senior citizen and pensioner (simulated)

**Brief:** 71, retired teacher. Pension account and fixed deposits with a cooperative bank. Son helps with phone apps.

How would you complain about an interest error on your FD?

“I would write a letter and give it at the branch. I want a stamped copy. A reference number on SMS would also do, if it comes immediately.”

Would you use the chat?

“If my son sets it up, maybe. Large text, please. And I want to talk to a person if it’s about money.”

#### 9. What the simulation suggests, hypothesis by hypothesis
