1. Executive Summary

Grievance Desk is a white-label complaints website and staff console for India's smaller banks. A customer lodges a complaint about any banking service, by form or by chatting with an AI assistant, gets a reference number straight away and tracks it. The bank's complaints officers work one shared queue in which every case runs on two clocks: the bank's own 24-hour resolution target, and RBI's rule that a bank must give its final reply within 30 days before the customer can go to the RBI Ombudsman. AI routes each case to the right team, and an escalation matrix raises it automatically within hours, long before Day 30. A monthly report gives the nodal officer the numbers the board and RBI ask for.

~600Tier 2 and 3 urban cooperative banks in our beachhead
56%of bank fraud cases are card and internet fraud
₹3.6 lakh/yrtarget price for a 25-branch bank, below one clerk's cost

Why we win: it is built around RBI's grievance rules, not adapted from a generic helpdesk. It is safe by design: OTPs, PINs, card numbers, Aadhaar and PAN are refused in the browser and again in the database. Fraud goes first. Every look at customer data is logged. Data stays in India. There is nothing to install, so a bank can go live in under two weeks.

Strategy in one line: Give a smaller bank a complaints desk it can trust and launch in under two weeks. It lets customers complain just by chatting, uses AI to route every case to the right team and resolve it within 24 hours, never past RBI's 30 days, keeps customers' secrets out of the system, and hands the nodal officer a board-ready report every month.

Beachhead: Tier 2 and 3 urban cooperative banks (5 to 60 branches) in Maharashtra and Gujarat, then small finance banks and the 28 merged regional rural banks. GTM motion: account-based, founder-led sales through cooperative-bank federations, core-banking vendors and auditors, backed by a free 60-day pilot. Business model: annual SaaS subscription by bank size, plus a setup fee. Launch: week of 18 January 2027, with 3 pilot banks live and 2 paying banks (₹6 lakh+ ARR) by 31 March 2027.

What we built for the capstone: a complete, working product covering three core flows end to end (lodge, track, staff resolution), with two AI features. New since Mini Project 3: a monthly MIS report with CSV and PDF export, a reviewer demo mode, and project documentation in CLAUDE.md.

2. Strategy and Opportunity

2.1 The problem

India's smaller banks face the same complaint rules as the largest banks: a named nodal officer, a final reply within 30 days, escalation to the RBI Ombudsman, and growing data-protection duties. But they run complaints on branch registers, a shared mailbox and an Excel sheet. Nobody has a live view of which cases are close to Day 30. Fraud complaints wait in the same pile as cheque-book requests. Customers email OTPs and Aadhaar copies. At inspection time, staff rebuild the history by hand. The nodal officer, who is personally accountable, finds out a case is late only when it has already escalated.

2.2 Why now

  • Complaints are rising fast. The RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme received 13,34,244 complaints in FY2024-25, up 13.55% from 11,75,075, and the disposal rate slipped from 95.1% to 93.1%. Loans (29.3%), credit cards (17.2%), mobile and electronic banking (16.9%) and deposit accounts (16.8%) lead. All four are in our service list.
  • Consolidation and tighter supervision. The number of UCBs fell to 1,457 by March 2025, with 7 mergers and 8 licence cancellations in the year, under a four-tier regulatory framework. RRBs were merged from 43 into 28 on 1 May 2025. Survivors must show they are well run.
  • Small UCB customers have no RBI fallback. The Ombudsman scheme covers UCBs only above ₹50 crore in deposits, so for many cooperative-bank customers, the bank's own desk is the only formal route.
  • Digital fraud. Card and internet fraud made up 13,516 of 23,953 bank fraud cases in FY2024-25. Victims must report within 3 working days for zero liability, so fraud complaints must be found and acted on the same day.
  • DPDP Act, 2023. Data minimisation, purpose-limited consent and access logging are becoming legal duties. Our product already does all three.
  • AI makes it affordable. An AI assistant can turn a customer's own words, in their own language, into a complete complaint, and AI-assisted development let a small team build a security-hardened product in weeks. That is what makes a price smaller banks can approve possible.

Aiming at 24 hours means a case reaches Day 30 only if several people have ignored it.

4.3 Where AI fits

Built with AI: the product was designed and coded with Claude Code on Supabase (hosted Postgres) and GitHub Pages. That's why a small team could build a production-grade, security-hardened app in weeks, and that speed is part of our cost advantage. AI in the product comes from two features, both built into the prototype. They run in a server-side function that calls Claude (Anthropic), so the API key never reaches the browser. Live demo: https://connect2soniasuresh-ui.github.io/MyComplaintsSystem/

AI featureWhat it doesWhy it matters
Built
1. AI Complaint Assistant (chatbot)		The customer types the problem in their own words, for example “I paid ₹2,500 by UPI on 2 Oct, money left my account but the shop didn't get it”. The assistant identifies the service and issue (UPI, debit-but-not-credited), pulls out the date, amount and UTR, asks only for what's missing, and shows a summary. One click fills in the complaint form; the customer checks it, adds their name and mobile, and submits it to get a reference number. It replies in the customer's language (English, Hindi or another Indian language) and fills in the form in English. Every message goes through the existing confidential-data guard, so if the customer types an OTP or PIN the assistant refuses it and explains why. It will never ask for one.Removes the hardest part of complaining (choosing among 12 services and about 50 issues), so customers drop off less and complaints are complete. This matters for rural and less tech-savvy customers.
Built
2. AI routing and auto-escalation		Routing: AI reads each complaint and sends it to one of nine teams (Cards & ATMs, Payments & UPI, Digital banking, Loans & recovery, Accounts & deposits, Branch operations, Insurance & investments, KYC & records, Fraud cell), using the service, the issue, the amount and the wording, and rates its risk as low, medium or high. It also catches likely fraud the customer filed under the wrong category. Escalation: a rule-based matrix, informed by the AI risk rating, escalates cases automatically. A fraud case, or one the AI rates high risk, goes to the team lead straight away, and fraud of ₹1 lakh or more goes to the Principal Nodal Officer. A case with no action for 4 hours goes to the team lead. A case open 12 hours against the 24-hour target goes to the Principal Nodal Officer, and at 20 hours to the Internal Ombudsman or GM. A customer's third complaint in 90 days goes to the Principal Nodal Officer. Levels never go down. Every routing and escalation is logged in the case history with the reason.Complaints reach the right officer in minutes, not days. Cases are escalated within hours, so breaches of the 30-day limit are prevented long before they happen, and the escalation chain RBI expects is followed automatically and recorded.

Safeguards (as built): the chatbot never sees the customer's name, mobile or email, because the customer types those into the form; messages containing an OTP, PIN, CVV, card, Aadhaar or PAN number are blocked before they reach the model; the AI routes each complaint only once and staff can re-route; rule-based routing and fraud flags apply even if the AI is unavailable; each user is rate-limited; every AI decision is recorded in the audit trail. Later roadmap: a weekly AI insight brief for the board and AI-drafted replies for officers.

6.4 Communication approach

  • Tone: calm, plain-language and trustworthy, like a senior compliance officer, not a tech startup. The product itself is written in simple English (“Tell us what went wrong”), and our marketing should sound the same.
  • Show it, don't describe it: the strongest sales moment is typing an OTP into the form and watching it refuse. Build every demo, video and webinar around that moment and the 24-hour clock.
  • Trust through peers: cooperative bankers buy what other cooperative bankers use. Federation endorsement and pilot-bank testimonials are the most important assets.
  • Responsible AI: “AI assists, a human decides.” The chatbot runs through the same confidential-data guard and never asks for an OTP or PIN. Officers can override any AI routing decision. Every routing and escalation is logged with its reason, and customers' contact details never go to the AI.
  • Vernacular: customer-facing collateral (posters, SMS) in Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati, Tamil and other languages for each pilot bank's region.

7. Pricing and Business Model

7.1 Business model

B2B white-label SaaS, annual subscription paid by the bank, priced by bank size (number of branches) with a generous complaint allowance, plus a one-time setup fee. End customers never pay.

Why price by branches: cooperative-bank boards understand and budget by branch count. It is stable and predictable, which avoids the bank worrying that “more complaints means a bigger bill”. It also tracks value, since more branches mean more complaints and more regulatory exposure. Per-user pricing would discourage banks from giving every officer and branch manager access.

7.2 Pricing rationale

  • Value created: one complaints clerk costs a UCB about ₹4–5 lakh a year. Grievance Desk removes manual register and Excel work, cuts “where is my complaint” calls, and helps avoid Ombudsman escalations, compensation awards and adverse inspection findings. We estimate ₹6–10 lakh a year of value for a 25-branch bank.
  • What customers pay elsewhere: generic helpdesks cost ₹1–3 lakh a year but need configuration and lack banking controls. Core-banking change requests and enterprise suites cost ₹10 lakh+.
  • Cost to serve: static hosting and hosted Postgres cost roughly ₹2–5k a month per bank, plus AI API costs of under ₹1 per complaint. That allows about 80% gross margin at prices cooperative banks can approve.
  • Price point: capture about 30–40% of the value, for an ROI of 3x or more and a price below the cost of one clerk.
