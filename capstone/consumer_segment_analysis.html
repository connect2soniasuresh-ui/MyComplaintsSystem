1. Summary

Grievance Desk has two customers. The buyer is a bank, and the person who feels the pain most is its Principal Nodal Officer, the officer RBI holds responsible for complaints. The end user is the bank's own customer, who needs to complain safely and know what is happening. The product only works if it wins both.

Our choices, in one paragraph. We target Tier 2 and Tier 3 urban cooperative banks (UCBs) with 5 to 60 branches first, roughly 600 banks, because they face rising RBI scrutiny and the 30-day complaint rule with small IT teams and paper or Excel processes. Small finance banks and the 28 newly merged regional rural banks are the second wave. Our primary persona is Rajesh Kulkarni, a 46-year-old Principal Nodal Officer at a 28-branch Pune UCB. He is accountable when a complaint goes past Day 30, but he has no live view of his cases (our product puts every case on a 24-hour clock) and spends a late night every month building the board report by hand. On the customer side we design for the least confident user: a semi-urban or rural customer, often a fraud victim, who may write in Hindi or Marathi and may be tempted to share an OTP.

Evidence: RBI's Ombudsman received 13.34 lakh complaints in FY2024-25, up 13.6% in a year, and loans, cards, mobile banking and deposits make up about 80% of them. Card and internet fraud is 56% of all bank fraud cases by count. Only 37.8% of Indians aged 15+ can do online banking. Each of these findings maps to a feature we built (Section 10).

Honest status of our research. This analysis is built on published RBI and government data, on the regulations that shape the buyer's job, and on our product walkthroughs. Our 15 to 20 interviews with nodal officers and CEOs are scheduled in the execution plan (W2 to W4). Every claim below is tagged so readers can see what is proven and what we still need to validate: D published data, R regulation or rule, H team hypothesis to test in interviews.

FindingEvidenceSo what for Grievance Desk
Complaints are rising fast. 13,34,244 complaints under the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme in FY2024-25, up 13.55% from 11,75,075 in FY2023-24. Disposal slipped from 95.1% to 93.1%.D RBI Ombudsman report 2024-25Volume is outgrowing manual processes. Every complaint that reaches RBI is one the bank failed to close itself.
Banks are the main target. Banks drew 81.5% of complaints handled by RBI Ombudsman offices; NBFCs 14.8%.D sameBanks first; NBFCs are a Year 2 adjacency.
Four categories dominate. Loans and advances 29.3%, credit cards 17.2%, mobile and electronic banking 16.9%, deposit accounts 16.8%.D sameAll four are among our 12 services. The issue list is weighted towards them.
Small UCBs have no RBI Ombudsman fallback. The scheme covers UCBs only with deposits of ₹50 crore or more. Complaints against UCBs at RBI were just 3,369 in FY2024-25 (about 1% of the total), down 11.3%.D RBI, via Indian CooperativeFor customers of smaller UCBs, the bank's own desk is the only formal route, so it has to work. Low numbers at RBI likely reflect coverage and awareness, not happy customers H.
UCBs are consolidating under tighter rules. 1,457 UCBs at March 2025 (down from 1,926 in 2004). Seven mergers and eight licence cancellations in 2024-25. A four-tier regulatory framework since December 2022; 57.5% of UCBs are Tier 1.D RBI Trend and Progress 2024-25Survivors are under pressure to look well run. Tier 2 and 3 banks (about 600) are big enough to pay and small enough to lack a CRM.
RRBs are now 28 large state banks. 43 RRBs merged into 28 from 1 May 2025, with 22,000+ branches in about 700 districts.D Ministry of FinanceFewer, larger RRBs mean bigger deals and one decision-maker per state: a strong second wave.
Fraud complaints are mostly digital. 23,953 bank fraud cases in FY2024-25; card and internet fraud was 13,516 of them (56%).D RBI Annual Report 2024-25Fraud must jump the queue, and the customer must be told to block first. RBI's zero-liability window is only 3 working days.
Many customers are not confident online. Only 37.8% of people aged 15+ can do online banking; rural households trail urban ones on internet access (71% vs 87%).D NSO CAMS 2022-23A 12-service form is hard for this group. They need a chat in their own words, plain language, and branch staff able to lodge on their behalf.
Breaches bring public penalties. RBI regularly publishes monetary penalties on cooperative banks, including for customer-liability and KYC lapses.D RBI press releasesThe nodal officer and CEO feel personal and reputational risk. Audit evidence is a buying driver.

Beachhead definition. RBI-regulated urban cooperative banks in Tier 2 or 3, with 5 to 60 branches, starting in Maharashtra and Gujarat, where most UCBs are. Typical profile H: 20 to 150 complaints a week, 1 to 10 staff handling them part-time, a core-banking system from an outside vendor, no CRM, and complaints tracked in branch registers, a shared mailbox and an Excel sheet.

4.1 Why this beachhead wins

  • Highest pain for the size of the team. The same 30-day rule and inspection expectations as a big bank, but no CRM and a small IT budget R.
  • Best product fit. Lockers, cheques, deposits and branch service, the services cooperative banks are known for, are all in our form. Large-bank CRMs are overkill.
  • Reachable as a group. State UCB federations and the national federation (NAFCUB) give us one door to hundreds of banks. Nodal officers' names are public on bank websites.
  • References compound. Cooperative bankers trust what other cooperative bankers use H, so three pilot banks in one state can unlock the rest.

6. Primary persona: Rajesh Kulkarni, Principal Nodal Officer

A composite built from the role's regulatory duties R and the segment profile D. Quotes are illustrative of the hypotheses we will test, not interview transcripts. They will be replaced with real quotes after W2 to W4.

Profile
Age 46. Principal Nodal Officer and Head of Customer Service at a Tier 3 urban cooperative bank in Pune: 28 branches, about 3.5 lakh customers, roughly 40 complaints a day across all channels, 4 people handling them part-time.

Background
Joined as a clerk 22 years ago and rose through branch operations. Confident with the core-banking system and Excel, cautious with new tech. His name and phone number are on the bank's website as nodal officer R, so escalations land on his desk personally. Reports to the CEO and presents to the board's Customer Service Committee every quarter.

A typical week
Collects complaint registers scanned by branches; reads the shared complaints mailbox; phones branch managers for updates; updates a master Excel sheet; chases fraud cases with the card team. Once a month he stays late to build the board report by hand.

Goals

  • No complaint crosses Day 30, and nothing reaches RBI
  • Pass the next RBI inspection without scrambling for evidence
  • Look in control in front of the CEO and the board
  • Protect customers from fraud, and from oversharing their secrets

Frustrations

  • “I find out a complaint is late when the customer calls the CEO.” H
  • “People email me their OTP and Aadhaar. I don't want that in my inbox.” H
  • “The board report takes me a whole evening every month.” H
  • “Our IT vendor quotes six months for anything new.” H

6.2 Jobs to be done

  • Functional: “When a complaint comes in through any channel, help me log it, resolve it within 30 days, and prove that we did.”
  • Risk: “Keep customers' secrets out of our systems, and get fraud cases to the right people today.”
  • Reporting: “At month end, give me the numbers the board and RBI ask for without a night in Excel.”
  • Emotional and social: “Let me face the board and RBI inspectors with confidence.”

7.1 End-user persona: Lakshmi Pawar

Age 52. Runs a small tailoring business in a town near Satara. Savings account and a gold loan with the cooperative bank for 15 years. Uses WhatsApp and UPI on her phone, mostly in Marathi. Her son helps with anything “official”.

Moment of need: she got a call from “the bank” asking her to confirm a code, and ₹18,000 left her account. She is frightened and embarrassed, and she doesn't know whether to go to the branch, call, or write.

Needs

  • Tell someone what happened, in her own words and language
  • Know what to do right now (block the card)
  • A number to quote and a way to check progress without visiting the branch again
  • Never be asked for a code again by anyone claiming to be the bank

Composite persona built from fraud and digital-skills data D. To validate with branch intercepts.

11.2 Interview guide for nodal officers (30 minutes)

  1. Walk me through the last complaint you handled, from the moment it came in to the final reply.
  2. Through which channels do complaints arrive? Roughly how many a week? Who logs them, and where?
  3. How do you know which complaints are close to 30 days? When did one last go past it, and what happened?
  4. How do fraud complaints reach you today? How fast?
  5. Have customers ever sent you an OTP, card number or Aadhaar copy? What did you do with it?
  6. Show me your last board or RBI complaints report. How long did it take?
  7. What did the last RBI inspection say about grievance handling?
  8. If you could change one thing about how complaints are handled, what would it be?
  9. (Demo.) What stands out? What worries you? Who else would need to see this?
  10. Rank these from most to least important: regulatory safety, price, ease for branch staff, data in India, peer recommendation, AI features.
  11. At what monthly price would this be too cheap to trust, a bargain, getting expensive, too expensive? Who approves a spend of that size?
